David Schell: Building with light

Undergraduate research on gate-tunable absorption in graphene microcavities, and computer-vision projects that turn a webcam into an input device.

Behind this page: a mesh of Mach–Zehnder interferometers, the building block of a programmable photonic processor. Each photon enters the mesh, is split across two paths at each coupler, picks up a phase shift from a heater, and interferes back together, with the phase shift tilting the balance of which port it emerges from.

Research

Gate-tunable critical coupling and absorption switching in an all-dielectric graphene–Bragg microcavity

Sole author · manuscript in preparation for ACS Photonics

A single sheet of graphene is placed inside an asymmetric TiO₂/SiO₂ Bragg cavity. Applying a gate voltage shifts the graphene's Fermi level, which changes how strongly it absorbs light. At one specific voltage the cavity reaches critical coupling and absorbs almost everything that hits it; move the voltage and the device switches back to mostly transparent.

I derived the device response with a two-port coupled-mode theory, modeled the gate electrostatics explicitly rather than hand-picking a Fermi level, computed graphene's conductivity from the full Kubo formalism, and verified everything against a transfer-matrix simulation I wrote from scratch in JavaScript.

Method
Two-port coupled-mode theory, Kubo conductivity, transfer-matrix simulation of the full 30-layer stack
Key result
Peak absorption ≈ 0.999 at the critical-coupling voltage, robust across a sweep of scattering rates
Why it's different
Fully dielectric mirrors (no metal), realistic voltage-to-Fermi-level electrostatics, and a two-port treatment where prior work used single-port models
Code
Physics engine and browser UI, Node.js validation suite; archived on GitHub with a Zenodo DOI
